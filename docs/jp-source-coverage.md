## Practice conditions

The corner heavy-Departure +15 tree begins after the shown 2MP > 236PP > st.HP > 214HP prerequisite. That earlier OD Stribog costs two Drive bars outside each follow-up loop. The +42 medium-Stribog jump-heavy-kick example actually lands guarding Ryu’s OD uppercut; it is not proof against every reversal. The OD-Departure starter spends four Drive bars in total, including OD Stribog and the portal placement.

Source captions that call an ender meterless do not make the complete OD starter free. Printed +F values are remaining frame advantage, not waits. Whiffed jabs remain visible in compact/copy notation. Delays are authored only when performed; no exact wait count is invented.

Medium 236MK is an overhead projectile in these recordings; heavy 236HK is the low branch. Generic source captions such as “MK or HK” are narrowed to the actual live glyph. OD Triglav and Departure button pairs are explicit because placement matters. Portal attacks, Amnesia bombs and Level 2 attacks happen automatically; they are distinguished from authored manual inputs.

Corner throw follow-ups name the exact prior throw and spacing. The shimmy depends on the demonstrated backdash-only standing-medium-kick spacing. Projectile-hit, trade and guarded examples have different conditions; the source’s separate guarded medium-projectile example reports -6, so successful hit conversions are not universally safe pressure. OD Amnesia responds to the actual recorded Ryu strike and uses automatic bombs through a back throw. Its +53 light-spike follow-up has a separate no-Drive dash/projectile loop.

The Ryu-SA1 guard punish performs raw Rush, two forward-heavy/medium-Stribog pickups, standing heavy, **OD Torbalan**, heavy Triglav and **normal heavy Departure**. The source leaves its ender as “any”; live kick glyphs and the final single heavy-punch portal input establish the actual route. The OD-uppercut side switch instead uses LP+HP OD Departure first and MP+HP OD Departure at the end.

Level 2 long carry uses ordinary dashes, not Drive Rush. The counter-Impact route absorbs one hit before the later Impact wins, then spends another Impact and Level 1 during Level 2; opponent Drive loss is visible, but no exact drain/Burnout threshold is promised. The guarded-target walk-throw route is pressure followed by a throw opening, not a continuous combo or unavoidable string. The six-Drive near-corner route enters Burnout in the recording and is labeled as a full commitment.
